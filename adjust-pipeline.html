Adjust reporting pipeline

A Python pipeline that pulls cohort data from Adjust and writes it into Google Sheets, so the team can monitor game growth and ad performance without manual exports.

Role
Prompt Engineer
Timeline
May 2025 – present
Tools
Python, Adjust, Google Sheets
Scope
Top 100 apps · 10 channels · 6 months · new-games dashboard

  2. What it collects
  3. How it works
  4. Built to not lose work

Challenge

To judge a game, you need to see more than installs. You need retention, time spent and revenue per user, broken down by channel and by month. The data lives in Adjust, and getting it by hand is slow and hard to repeat, while the team works in Google Sheets.

What it collects

The pipeline first finds the top 100 apps by installs over the last 30 days. Then, for each app, channel and month, it gathers six months of cohort data:

  • Installs and retention from day 1 up to day 30.
  • Time spent per user across the first 30 days.
  • Revenue per user, both all sources and in-app purchases only.
  • ROAS from day 0 to day 30.

It covers ten channels: Applovin, Organic, Google Ads, Unity Ads, Apple, Adjoe, Tapjoy, Aura from Unity, Mistplay and puzzleplay, in both a global and a US-only version.

How it works

Built to not lose work

Long API pulls fail. So the pipeline was designed around that:

  • Save as you go. Each channel is written to its own tab as soon as it finishes, so a crash halfway keeps everything already done.
  • Stay within API limits. Each metric group is split into two halves (days 0–15 and 16–30) and requested with up to three attempts.
  • Stop on demand. Creating a stop file halts the run cleanly after the current channel.
  • Visible progress. Every step logs its timing and row count, which makes failures easy to trace.

New-games dashboard

Google Sheets feeds a dashboard for daily monitoring of new games. It is built around one question a UA team asks every morning: is this game paying back what we spend on it?

What it shows

  • Overview of all new games. A launch calendar (first day each game has data), a heat map of activity by day, week or month, and key insights generated from the data, most urgent first.
  • One game at a time. Filter by game, by range (7, 14, 30 days or all) and by channel. Headline numbers are installs split into paid and organic, cost and CPI, revenue, ROAS against the 100% break-even line, and profit or loss.
  • Profit or loss by cohort day. Revenue from day 0 to day 15 minus cost, paid channels only, so a loss is visible before it grows.
  • Creative cost versus revenue. Each paid creative is a dot. Above the break-even diagonal is profit, below is loss, and dot size is installs.
  • Campaign, ad group, creative. A table you can expand and sort, with day-1 retention alongside.

Design choices

  • Organic is kept apart from paid. Organic installs have no cost, so profit and ROAS are calculated on paid channels only.
  • Missing cost is marked, not hidden. Where cost had to be estimated from ROAS, the figure carries a "~" so nobody mistakes it for a reported number.
  • Colour never works alone. Profit and loss also carry ▲ and ▼ symbols.
